Method and limitations

A radar satellite sees ships through cloud and at night, and every large vessel is meant to broadcast where it is. Comparing the two is useful, but the comparison fails in specific, knowable ways. This page states them, because a surveillance tool that hides its failure modes is worse than no tool.

Pipeline

What the radar can and cannot see

  • ✓Works through cloud and at night. A ship’s metal corners bounce the signal straight back, so it appears bright on dark sea.
  • ⚠Pixels are 10 m but true resolution is nearer 20 m. Boats under roughly 15–20 m are frequently missed altogether.
  • ⚠In strong wind the sea itself brightens, so detection degrades exactly when small vessels are least safe.
  • ⚠A moving ship is imaged away from its true position, by several hundred metres. Corrected before matching, never perfectly.
  • ⚠A pass every few days is a snapshot, not a track. Nothing here shows where a vessel went.

Small boats are also carried by people crossing the Aegean. Publishing their positions could put lives at risk, which is why individual points below the size floor are never shown.

Rules this site publishes under

Delay
Nothing appears until it is 72 hours old, which removes any real-time tracking value.
Excluded areas
Naval bases, military ports and announced exercise areas are cut before publication.
Size floor
Individual detections are shown only at or above 30 m. Anything smaller appears as a count per 10 km cell.
No identities
Vessel names, MMSI and call signs are never published, even where a match is confident.
Wording
A detection is “unmatched” or “no AIS received” — never “dark”, “suspicious” or “illegal”. Most have ordinary explanations.
